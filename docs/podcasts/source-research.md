# Podcast sources and editorial selection

## Recommended search portfolio

The podcast slot should prioritize practical AI use by knowledge workers and non-technical people. Six sources form a useful initial portfolio: The AI Daily Brief, Everyday AI, The Artificial Intelligence Show, AI for Humans, Practical AI, and Latent Space. TWIML supplies an additional technical fallback. These are editorial judgments about fit, based on publisher descriptions, episode catalogs, and accessible episode notes reviewed September 10, 2026. They are not audience rankings or blanket endorsements of every episode.

The AI Daily Brief is a mandatory daily search source. Mandatory checking does not mean automatic selection. Every episode still needs substantive relevance, verifiable identity, accessible content, and a clear reason to occupy the ninth slot. The slot follows six articles and two videos. There is no maximum episode duration, including when the podcast is distributed as a YouTube video.

## Source assessments

### The AI Daily Brief

The publisher maintains dated editions and links to listening platforms. Its recent catalog includes agent-workflow education as well as model news and enterprise analysis. The September 3 edition, “Agentic Loops for Knowledge Workers,” is particularly well aligned: it addresses completion tests and iterative workflows for non-software work. This makes the show a strong discovery source for the brief’s audience. A dated publisher page is also useful for resolving identity when platform listings differ. [1][2]

The show includes sponsors and its own educational offerings. Selection should favor the instructional substance and omit sales claims. For instance, a program launch can be relevant, but the existence of a promotional episode is not sufficient evidence of effectiveness. Distinguish the host’s analysis from facts verified in original documentation. A show homepage, a subscription link, and an episode link must be labeled differently.

### Everyday AI

Everyday AI explicitly targets practical workplace and business use. The reviewed catalog includes an episode about scheduling AI work in Claude, Gemini, and ChatGPT. That is a useful fit for readers who want repeatable outcomes without becoming developers. The publisher provides an episode catalog and a substantial set of podcast-platform links. [3][4]

The strongest selections will be concrete workflows with clear prerequisites and limitations. Claims about plan eligibility, available features, permissions, and scheduling should be checked against current vendor documentation before the brief repeats them as facts. Forecast episodes should remain lower priority than usable instruction or evidence-backed analysis. Do not infer a publication date from a recent crawl or an episode number.

### The Artificial Intelligence Show

The current publisher site describes a weekly show focused on business and career implications, hosted by Paul Roetzer and Mike Kaput. It provides show notes and links to Apple, Spotify, and YouTube. The older Marketing AI Institute podcast address redirects to this newer destination, so searches should include the current site. [5]

This is a good source for management-oriented readers and consultants. Broad news roundups require an additional check for duplication: the podcast should contribute interpretation, a decision framework, or practical context beyond the six articles. A long episode can qualify, but the brief should explain which portions are most useful when reliable chapter information is available.

### AI for Humans

The official site presents an accessible show hosted by Gavin Purcell and Kevin Pereira, with audio and YouTube distribution. The accessible Apple catalog includes hands-on creative and tool demonstrations. This supports its use as an approachable discovery source, particularly for readers interested in creative work and non-developer experimentation. [6][7]

The entertainment format makes episode-level filtering essential. A striking demo alone does not establish repeatable productivity, accuracy, or affordability. Select episodes whose useful portions map clearly to the brief’s focus areas, and explain the practical work outcome in plain language. Exclude celebrity imitation, spectacle, and unrelated entertainment when they dominate the episode.

### Practical AI

Practical AI describes its scope as productive real-world AI implementation for technical and business audiences. The current site lists “Less about Models; More about Architecture,” dated September 3, 2026, with a runtime of 45:56. That is a useful example of a technical fallback that can still inform decisions about reliable systems. The earlier Changelog address redirects to the current site. [8][9]

Prefer episodes about deployment lessons, evaluation, data quality, guardrails, interoperability, and agent design. Explain engineering terminology and state the prerequisites. The current subscription page was accessible, but its extracted text did not expose a usable RSS address; no feed URL should be invented from that absence.

### Latent Space and TWIML

Latent Space identifies itself as a technical AI-engineering publication and podcast covering agents, infrastructure, models, and related systems. Its about page supplies platform-discovery links and a detailed topic inventory. It is valuable when a technical episode contributes evidence or implementation detail that more accessible shows do not provide. [10]

TWIML’s catalog includes episodes on agent security, RAG, evaluations, context engineering, and production systems. Those categories map closely to the brief’s reliability focus. The catalog also includes many other machine-learning subjects, so source reputation cannot substitute for topic alignment. Technical fallbacks should be selected only after checking practical knowledge-worker candidates. [11]

## Search and verification procedure

Check the mandatory show and the core accessible sources on every publishing run. Search publisher websites and dated notes first, then official YouTube channels and podcast platforms. Discover RSS feeds only from publisher links or page metadata, and verify that the feed belongs to the same show. Search beyond the initial portfolio when the current candidates are weak; the portfolio is a starting set, not a closed allowlist.

Prefer the preceding 24 hours, then expand to seven days and, when necessary, 30 days. State the expanded window in the published item. A strong practical episode may be preferable to a newer episode with poor audience fit. Do not quietly recycle the same episode: compare the canonical episode URL, publisher identity, platform episode IDs, title, guest, and publication date against the preceding 30 days and both video slots.

Record the sources checked, candidate titles, dates, audience fit, accessible evidence, inclusion decision, and rejection reasons in a dated podcast search record. Rank eligible episodes by practical value, focus alignment, verifiability, novelty, and then freshness. These podcast judgments must not alter the six-article allocation or protected article scoring dimensions.

Verify the episode’s title, show, host or guest, publication date, and subject matter through publisher notes, a transcript, or accessible audio/video. Do not claim to have listened when only notes were inspected. Treat show notes as evidence about the episode’s contents, not independent confirmation of every factual assertion it contains. Verify material product or research claims separately, or attribute them as the speaker’s analysis.

A podcast can exist in audio and video versions with different advertisement lengths or edits. Never apply a YouTube runtime to an audio edition without checking that they match. Duration is informative rather than a gate. If an exact runtime cannot be established, show it as unverified; do not reject an otherwise useful episode solely for that reason.

## Episode identity, reader experience, and measurement

Each selected episode receives one immutable item ID and one permanent Daily AI Brief podcast page. All listening destinations on that page belong to that single item. Sharing sends the permanent brief page, where recipients can read the summary, choose a listening platform, rate usefulness, and return home. It does not send the original reader’s selected rating.

Preserve the current share semantics: one Share-button initiation increments the counter once, including when the subsequent share dialog is canceled. Do not count choosing a destination as another share. Display the same server-backed share count on the daily brief and permanent page. Retain ratings locally during a transport outage and retry through the existing privacy-minimized mechanism.

The Command Center should include podcasts in Overview, Pipeline, Editorial Intelligence, Readers, QA content coverage, the content explorer, and System Health. The archive index and feeds should retain the podcast’s type and stable URL. Historical editions without podcasts retain their original expected item count. Podcast ratings add to all-content totals, while article-only learning sufficiency and owner-approval protections remain separate.

## Launch episode and evidence limits

The launch selection is “Agentic Loops for Knowledge Workers,” published September 3, 2026. Publisher notes and the linked Spotify episode establish the title and instructional topic. It is included in the September 9 edition as a clearly labeled seven-day fallback. The brief summarizes its main teaching point and proposes a workshop application; it does not repeat unverified usage statistics from the episode. [2][12]

The publisher lists an approximately 57-minute episode, while search surfaced a different YouTube duration without a verifiable direct episode URL. The canonical record therefore leaves exact runtime unknown and supplies the verified website and Spotify episode links. The YouTube channel remains in the daily search portfolio. This is preferable to presenting a channel subscription link as an episode-specific watch link.
